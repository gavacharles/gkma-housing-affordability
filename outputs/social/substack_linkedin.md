# Substack post

**Title:** Priced out of the formal market: who can afford a home in Greater Kampala?
**Subtitle:** Early findings from a new parish-level map of housing affordability

Ask anyone in Kampala about housing and you'll hear the same thing: prices keep rising, and it's getting harder to find somewhere decent to live at a price you can afford. What we've lacked is a map of *where* housing is priced beyond local incomes, and by how much.

National figures don't show this. The housing deficit, mortgage penetration and average rents all describe the country as a whole, not the neighbourhood you'd actually live in.

For my PhD at the University of Johannesburg, I've been building that map. The study brings together about 7,800 online property listings across the Greater Kampala Metropolitan Area, plus Census 2024 and household survey data from the Uganda Bureau of Statistics. It estimates, area by area, what share of households could afford the typical home on offer.

Three early findings stand out.

**1. Most households are priced out of the formal rental market.** For a typical listed 1–2 bedroom home, about 97% of households in Kampala, 87% in Wakiso and 77% in Mukono would have to spend more than 30% of their income on rent. That's the usual affordability threshold. Even in the most affordable areas, most households fall short.

**2. The online market is almost entirely titled property.** Fewer than 1% of land listings are kibanja, yet a large share of Greater Kampala sits on untitled or informal land. What's advertised online is a small, formal slice of the housing market. Most households live somewhere else entirely: household survey data show most Kampala renters live in single rooms.

**3. What drives prices changes across the city.** Spatial machine-learning models show that the factors behind prices, such as neighbourhood wealth, plot size and access to the Entebbe Expressway, don't carry the same weight everywhere. A one-size-fits-all housing policy will miss that.

Why does this matter? Housing policy in Uganda is increasingly focused on "affordable housing". But affordable to whom, and where? If the formal market serves only a small upper segment of households, then new supply priced for that market won't touch the needs of most families. A parish-level picture lets planners, lenders and policymakers target interventions where the gap is widest.

These are early results. The full study, with parish-level maps and detailed methods, is being prepared for peer review. I'll share it here when it's published.
