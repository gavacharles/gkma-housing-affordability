# Substack post

**Title:** Priced out of the formal market: who can afford a home in Greater Kampala?
**Subtitle:** A new map of housing affordability, built from 10,643 online listings

Ask anyone in Kampala about housing and you'll hear the same thing: prices keep rising, and it's getting harder to find somewhere decent to live at a price you can afford. What we've lacked is a map of *where* housing is priced beyond local incomes, and by how much.

National figures don't show this. The housing deficit, mortgage penetration and average rents all describe the country as a whole, not the neighbourhood you'd actually live in.

For my PhD at the University of Johannesburg, I've built that map. The study brings together 10,643 online property listings from across the Greater Kampala Metropolitan Area (2025–26) with Census 2024 and household survey data from the Uganda Bureau of Statistics. It asks, area by area, what share of households could afford the typical home on offer.

Four findings stand out.

**1. Almost everyone is priced out of the typical listed rental.** The median listed one- to two-bedroom rent in Greater Kampala is UGX 900,000 a month. At the usual rule of 30% of income, 97.5% of households could not afford it: 99% in Kampala, 94% in Wakiso and 87% in Mukono. The typical household would have to spend more than its entire income on that rent.

**2. The finding doesn't depend on the 30% rule.** Even if households spent 80% of their income on rent, 78% would still be priced out. Asked a different question — could they pay the rent and still afford basic needs? — about three in five households would be pushed below the poverty line.

**3. Buying with a mortgage is out of reach almost everywhere.** On typical Ugandan mortgage terms (about 18% interest, a 30% deposit, 20 years), the median listed house or apartment (UGX 470 million) would need a household income about 27 times the median.

**4. The online market is a small, formal slice of the city.** Fewer than 1% of land listings are kibanja, and only 21 of 3,914 houses for sale are described as untitled. Most households live in housing that never appears online: household survey data show most Kampala renters live in single rooms.

Why does this matter? Housing policy in Uganda is increasingly focused on "affordable housing". But affordable to whom, and where? If the formal market serves only a small upper segment of households, then new supply priced for that market won't reach most families. Mapping the gap at district and sub-county level lets planners, lenders and policymakers see where it is widest.

A note on the numbers: they compare *asking* prices of listed homes with *modelled* household incomes, and they describe the formal market, not informal rentals. The full study, with detailed methods, is being prepared for peer review. I'll share it here when it's published.
