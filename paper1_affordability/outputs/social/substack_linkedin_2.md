# Substack post (2)

**Title:** Just move further out? Chasing an affordable rent across Greater Kampala
**Subtitle:** Rents fall fast as you leave the city centre. Not fast enough.

There's a piece of advice every Kampala renter has heard: if the city is too expensive, move further out. Kira, Namugongo, Seeta, Mukono — the rent drops, the commute grows, and somewhere along the road the numbers are supposed to work.

So I followed that advice, using data. My PhD research at the University of Johannesburg brings together 10,643 online property listings from across Greater Kampala (2025–26) with UBOS Census 2024 and household survey data. For each area, it compares the typical listed one- to two-bedroom rent with the incomes of the households who actually live there.

Here's the journey.

**Stop 1: Central Division, Kampala.** The median listed 1–2 bedroom rent is UGX 1.5 million a month. The median household here earns about UGX 786,000. The rent is almost *twice* its income. At the familiar rule of spending no more than 30% of income on housing, more than 99% of households here could not afford it.

**Stop 2: Kira Division, Wakiso.** North-east of the city, beyond the Northern Bypass, the median rent falls to UGX 870,000 — and incomes are actually a little higher than in Central Division, at about UGX 879,000. The typical rent now roughly equals the typical household's entire income. At 30% of income, 91% are priced out.

**Stop 3: Goma Division, Mukono.** This is the most affordable sub-county in the study. The median rent is UGX 400,000; the median household earns about UGX 669,000. Between Central Division and Goma, rent has fallen by almost three-quarters, while income has fallen by only about 15%. That is a big improvement.

And still, 79% of households in Goma could not afford the typical listed rental at 30% of income.

**Why the advice runs out**

Moving out works in one sense: rent as a share of income falls from about 190% in Central Division to about 60% in Goma. But 60% of income is still twice the 30% benchmark. In the most affordable area measured, the typical household would still need to hand over more than half of what it earns to rent the typical home on offer.

A fairer test is not a percentage at all: after paying the rent, is there enough left for food, transport, school fees and the rest? By that measure, about half of Goma's households still could not rent the typical listed home and meet their basic needs. Across Greater Kampala, about three in five households would be pushed below the poverty line by the typical listed rent.

Buying doesn't rescue the strategy either. The most affordable sub-county to buy in is Nansana, and even there, on typical mortgage terms (about 18% interest, a 30% deposit, 20 years), the median listed house would need a household income about five times the local median.

**What this tells us**

Distance lowers the price, but it doesn't close the gap, because what's listed online in every suburb is priced for the same narrow, formal market. Most households live in housing that never makes it onto a property portal — single rooms, informal rentals, homes built bit by bit on kibanja land.

That matters for policy. If "affordable housing" means new formal units priced like today's listings, moving them to the edge of the city won't bring them within reach of most families. The price of what gets built has to change, not just its location.
